*Written in 2026-10-16 08:45:42*

Seriously, **this era of social media is just crazy**!

I have truly never seen an era where the emojis people "drop" on a post could hold such power, where the comments left on a post could transform the person who shared it, or leave them with such profound psychological distress

Because of these emojis and comments, people might be driven to do anything, even harm themselves or recklessly undertake some insane challenge

Please note: I am **not** saying that everyone acts this way, but if I were to claim that **no one** does, I would be lying

I remember the early days of the Internet, what a wonderful time that was. We had games on MS-DOS and Windows 98, powered by Voodoo3 graphics cards and SoundBlaster sounds cards, we spent afternoons playing PES on a PS2 at the local gaming shop... We used to go out, talk, make friends, socialize, play games together, and discover new things side-by-side

Now, when people get home, everyone retreats into their own phone, scrolling through TikTok or social media, sometimes without exchanging a single word all evening. Gradually, people are becoming increasingly distant, even within their own homes, let alone venturing out to make new friends

People are constantly fixated on things like, *"Wow, your post has so many likes!"* or *"Why are people saying this in the comments? What should I do?"* and when the reactions are negative, they are left feeling guilty and deeply troubled. It is true that when you make a mistake, people may look at you with hatred. However, some individuals exploit this to smear others, even when those people haven’t done anything significant or caused any harm to them

I believe social media does have its merits: it allows you to gauge what others think of you, and it enables people to connect and share their personal opinions and perspectives more freely

Yet, placing too much emphasis on this can be detrimental. While some people genuinely want to see you improve, others who hiding behind a facade of intellect or morality, may deliberately try to tear you down. If you haven’t done anything wrong, haven’t broken any civil, criminal, or federal laws, and haven’t picked fights or made enemies, **then you really shouldn’t worry about such comments**

Another issue I want to highlight is the prevalence of **fake news** and **scams**. Many social media pages, even those with a "verified" (blue) checkmark, post false information or sensationalist content just to drive traffic, there are also many fraudulent job postings. Despite numerous warnings, people often ignore them, thinking like *"Oh, I’m well aware, there’s no way I’d fall for a scam!"* and when the situation actually arises, they end up falling into the trap and getting deceived

And posts containing fake news often attract more attention and engagement, effectively generating a social impact based on claims whose veracity remains unverified, just look back at the period when the COVID-19 pandemic was raging globally, and you will see this clearly

In this era of social media, I find the landscape to be a chaotic mix, riddled with pitfalls. High-quality content is often **suppressed** by the platforms's algorithms, which **limit** its reach. Instead, these algorithms **prioritize** content that is controversial or designed to incite conflict. And gradually, people find themselves drawn into a death spiral:

1. They open social media, only to be confronted by a highly controversial post
2. They read the comments and become irritated
3. They post a comment to vent their frustration or share their personal opinion
4. Others jump in to argue or hurl insults (this is **real**, and happens far more often than you might think!)
5. Feeling that the other person has misunderstood them, sensing a "loss" in the argument, or being subjected to personal attacks, they become even more incensed
6. They post more and more, desperate to "win" the argument
7. The algorithm detects high levels of engagement on the post
8. The algorithm then prioritizes the post, pushing it out to an even wider audience

These are the very business models that social media platforms use to keep users hooked! They know it’s harmful, yet they continue to do it, simply because the profits are just too massive

Just hearing about it is exhausting. This has bothered me for a long time, ever since I was still using Meta’s Facebook, and today, I’m finally voicing these thoughts

Of course, walking away from the social platforms used by those around you is difficult, just look at LINE in Japan, KakaoTalk in South Korea, or WeChat in China. When everyone in your social circle and the society you live in uses a particular platform or ecosystem, detaching yourself from it can feel like self-imposed isolation

So, how can this actually be resolved? I have also found myself in a similar situation, and here is what I did, perhaps you might find it helpful:

- Most social media platforms today consist of two main components: messaging and a news feed (much like Messenger and Facebook) or, in the case of "super apps," even more features. **Ultimately, these are just various functions bundled together to form a complete ecosystem. Therefore, you should identify which specific features you actually use on a daily basis. For instance, if you need to make payments and communications, related messages, simply focus on using only those specific functions, payments and messaging**
- Next, practice the habit of avoiding news feeds or other social media app features that you don’t actually use. I know habits are hard to break, so take it one step at a time
- If you still wish to use a social media platform, consider open-source, community-driven alternatives, for example, BlueSky or Blind or Mastodon, Fediverse or Nostr (No, I am not advertising these apps. These are simply options I am providing for your reference)
- If possible, delete the social media app entirely (provided it exists as a standalone application). This will be the most effective way to support your progress
- Once you have established this habit, nurture it until it becomes an automatic reflex. Do not reinstall the app, as doing so would undo the new habit you have worked to build

I think the current era of social media is truly insane, and what’s most concerning is that events unfolding online now frequently escalate into real world actions. A single remark intended as a joke, a seemingly trivial comment, or a hostile exchange in a comment thread can lead people to resort to actual bloodshed, inflict harm on others, and, at times, even cause damage to the broader community in the real world. There are also many other issues, for instance, online abuse can drive victims into depression or lead them to take desperate, harmful actions against themselves or others

I believe we truly need, well, perhaps "enacting a new law" is a bit strong (though if it’s necessary, then so be it) to take more decisive action regarding social media platforms. These platforms should also implement stricter policies to address these issues. Most importantly, they shouldn't rely 100% on AI for indiscriminate filtering a method prone to errors and oversight. Instead, they should employ a human-in-the-loop approach, combining human judgment with AI to handle these matters

At the same time, everyone should remember to keep a clear head when consuming information on social media. We are no longer living in an era where one can turn to social media to gain knowledge, on the contrary, you now need to possess prior knowledge just to navigate social media safely

In this day and age, people engage in less (though not entirely absent) face-to-face interaction, which creates more opportunities for fraud. At the same time, this shift makes it more difficult for individuals to express their emotions when meeting others in person. A sad state of affairs!

Wishing you all a wonderful and healthy day and stay alert!
